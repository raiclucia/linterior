# Lucia's Spaces

Build a portfolio website for L'Interior, the interior design studio of
Lucia Raić, based in Zagreb, Croatia.

The brand name L'Interior appears in the header, footer, page titles and
metadata. Lucia's own name appears on the About page, in the site's writing
(first person — "I measure", "I design"), and as the signature on the contact
page. The studio is the brand; she is the person behind it.

GOAL
The site should convince potential clients (mostly private homeowners and small
commercial spaces) that Lucia is thorough, professional and worth contacting.
The two things that matter most: the project gallery and a clear explanation of
how she works.

Use the copy below exactly as written. Do not invent additional marketing text,
taglines or filler paragraphs beyond what is specified here.

4. PROCESS

  Page label: PROCESS
  Heading: How I work
  Intro: Interior design goes wrong in predictable ways — decisions made too
  late, drawings the contractor can't build from, a result nobody pictured.
  These five steps exist to prevent that. Each one finishes before the next
  one starts.

  01 — Measuring
  I come to the space and measure it myself: walls, ceiling heights, window
  openings, installations, everything that's already there and can't move.
  Existing conditions get photographed and documented. Every decision after
  this point is based on real dimensions rather than assumptions, which is why
  it's worth doing properly the first time.

  02 — Floor plan
  A technical drawing of the layout, usually in several versions. I test
  different arrangements and check how you'd actually move through the space —
  where you drop your keys, where the light falls in the afternoon, whether two
  people can pass in the kitchen. We keep revising until the plan fits how you
  live, not how a catalogue thinks you live.

  03 — Moodboard
  Materials, colours, textures and references collected in one place: floor
  finishes, wall treatments, joinery, hardware, textiles. We agree the direction
  here, before anything is modelled or ordered. It's the cheapest possible point
  to change your mind, and most people do.

  04 — 3D visualization
  A photorealistic render of the finished space from the angles you'll actually
  see it from. You get to walk through the room before it exists and say what
  you don't like — the tone of the wood, the height of a shelf, the position of
  a light. Changes at this stage cost nothing. Changes after the joinery is cut
  cost a great deal.

  05 — Documentation handoff
  Complete technical documentation for the people building it: dimensioned
  drawings, joinery details, electrical and lighting plans, material and finish
  schedules. Your contractor gets a set they can price accurately and build
  from without calling me every second day. What was visualised is what gets
  built.

  Closing block
    Heading: What it costs and how long it takes
    Text: Scope varies too much for a fixed price list. A small apartment
    typically runs six to ten weeks from first measurement to final
    documentation; larger projects take longer. After a first conversation I'll
    send you a written proposal with a fixed fee and a schedule, so you know
    both before committing to anything.

DESIGN DIRECTION
Editorial and quiet, not flashy. Let the photography carry the page. Generous
white space, wide margins, large images. Warm off-white or bone background
(#FAF8F5-ish), near-black text, one muted accent (terracotta, sage, or clay).
Elegant serif for headings (Cormorant Garamond, Fraunces or similar), clean
sans-serif for body text (Inter or Neue Montreal). Small uppercase letter-spaced
labels for section titles and navigation. Minimal borders — use spacing to
separate things instead of lines.

Subtle scroll-triggered fade-ins on images and section headings. No heavy
animation, no gradients, no drop shadows, no rounded card look.

BRANDING
Wordmark: "L'Interior" set in the heading serif, letter-spaced, no logo icon.
Use it at a restrained size in the top-left of the header — it should read as a
mark, not a banner. Keep the apostrophe as a proper typographic apostrophe (').
On the home page hero, the wordmark can appear larger with the uppercase line
beneath it. Favicon: the letter L in the same serif on the off-white background.

TECHNICAL
- Fully responsive; mobile is a priority since clients will share the link.
- Sticky minimal header with the wordmark on the left and nav on the right.
- Fast image loading with lazy loading and proper aspect ratios so nothing jumps.
- Use placeholder images throughout — I'll replace them with real project photos.
- Semantic HTML and alt text on every image.
- SEO basics: page titles in the format "Page — L'Interior", meta descriptions
  mentioning both L'Interior and Lucia Raić, Open Graph tags with a hero image.
